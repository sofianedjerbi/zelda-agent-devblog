Devlogan AI learning to finish any Zelda, on one Mac

Goal: an AI that finishes any top-down Zelda, on one Mac, with local models only. It must learn skills, not memorise a game. The brain is the same for every Zelda; each game needs a small adapter that reads its memory (position, room map, health, items).

The number that counts: dungeons it has never seen

The goal is an agent that knows how to play, not one that got one dungeon solved. So the first number here is no longer Zelda 1. It is how many dungeons the same agent solves when it has never seen them and I have never looked at them: {{ site.data.unseen.total }} generated dungeons with keys, locked doors, shutters, switches, enemies, item rooms and a boss door.

The last line is the big one. For two days I wrote the rules that rank what the agent does next, and three more rules in a row had just failed. So I stopped: now the agent plays thousands of training dungeons, takes a random option a quarter of the time, and every choice is scored by what followed it: a new room, a key, a way opened, the dungeon solved, or death. A small network learns to predict that from a description of the option that names no game and no dungeon. Five minutes of this beat everything I had written: 64% to 80%, in half the steps, on dungeons it has never seen.

The fourth line is the first thing the agent learned for itself about how to play. It played 2,000 other dungeons and kept count: for each look of thing (it sees that two things look alike, never what they are called) and each action, how often something happened. It found that one look answers to pulling, another to the action button, that two looks often hold something, and that the sword does nothing to any of them. On dungeons it has never seen it now tries the right action first and skips the useless ones.

Of the ones it fails, it dies in {{ site.data.unseen.died }} and runs out of steps in {{ site.data.unseen.out_of_steps }}. With three times the steps: {{ site.data.unseen.pct_long }}%. These dungeons are a simple grid world, not a real game: a good score here is needed, not enough. It started at {{ site.data.unseen.start }}% on 13 Nov. What the agent learns by itself today: its map, which ground is wall or door, which kinds of wall can open, what hurts, where each room crossing worked, and (in these dungeons) what each kind of thing answers to and which option to take next. What I wrote by hand: how goals are ranked, the sword rule, the thresholds. From now on a change counts only if this number goes up, and the real games do not go down.

Fighting

The whole fighting skill is one rule: when an enemy that can hurt Link is in the sword's line, strike. It is not learned and it does not dodge. I picked it with a fast test: 240 saved dangerous moments outside and 240 in the dungeon, 150 actions each.

In full games the rule cut game overs from 6.2 to 4.4 per 10,000 steps and hits from 33.8 to 24.1 (now 5.0 and 27.2). What does not work yet: dodging (see below), rooms with many enemies, and the boss (0 of 16 games reach it). Trained reflexes and backing off were both tried and rejected, see IDEAS.

Shots and movement

The agent avoided enemies but walked into their shots. I measured it: outside, at least 24 of 85 hits come from a shot (a rock), not from touching an enemy. The agent saw a shot only as a dot, with no speed and no direction.

It can now see movement: where each object is, minus where it was 8 frames earlier. Anything fast and straight that will reach Link within 4 steps is a shot, and he steps out of its line. Nothing in the code says what a shot looks like.

Neither is a gain, so both stay off. Seeing movement is not enough: a shot lives 3 to 12 decisions and the agent decides only every 16 to 48 frames. My fast fight test said deciding twice as often would halve the deaths (22, 49, 62 game overs of 240 when deciding every 8, 16, 32 frames); full games did not agree. Next: one death outside, frame by frame, to see what really hits Link.
